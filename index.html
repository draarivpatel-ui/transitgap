Methodology and lessons

How the loop is modeled

A volunteer loop becomes one more two-way line in the same street and transit graph the bus plan uses. Dijkstra then finds each resident's fastest trip, so walking to a pickup, waiting, riding and transferring to a bus all come from one search.

  1. Leg minutesceil(blocks × block meters / 450) + 1
  2. Round trip2 × sum of legs + 2 × 4 min turnaround
  3. Headwayround trip / vehicles
  4. Expected waitheadway / 2
  5. Access score1 / (1 + mean trip minutes / 30)
  6. Walk shedwalk to the nearest pickup ≤ 10 min

How the suggestion is chosen

Every destination is tried with every single pickup and every pair of pickups. Each candidate is scored by the service pairs that transit-dependent and senior residents newly reach within 30 minutes, plus a large bonus for each neighborhood brought back within 45 minutes of the hospital, minus the round-trip minutes.

Lessons from building it

  • Treating the shuttle as one more line meant transfers to buses appeared with no special code, and the same tests that guard the bus model guard the shuttle.
  • A loop can raise total coverage while the access Gini gets worse, as the suggested rail city loop does. The planner shows both numbers instead of hiding the trade.
  • Ranking by the share of residents who are transit-dependent or senior moved the rural suggestion from the biggest towns to the hamlets farthest from the hospital.
  • Seats per priority resident is the honest capacity check: a loop that reaches thousands of residents with a few hundred seats a week needs booking rules before it needs more drivers.

Limits

All cities, populations and services are synthetic. Gains hold only inside the service window. Fares, booking, detours, wheelchair loading time and driver no-shows are not modeled. The brief is a starting point for a conversation with neighbors and the transit agency, not a forecast.

    Metric definitions and planning-model unit costs

    Weighted access delta is the scenario minus baseline mean access score, weighted by population. Equity lift combines the Gini reduction with the bottom-quintile access gain and is reported per million dollars of annual package cost. The 20:20 ratio divides top-quintile mean access by bottom-quintile mean access; the Atkinson index uses inequality aversion 0.5. The Palma index compares the richest ten percent with the poorest forty. Dependent coverage counts zero-car, low-income and senior residents.

    Unit costs: $90 per bus-hour, 365 service days per year, $42,000 per lane-kilometre per year, $150,000 fixed annual cost per new line, $6,000 per added shelter per year. All are planning-model rates for comparing scenarios, not real-agency figures. A scenario saved in the address bar hash reloads the same inputs; the signed JSON export hashes the canonical inputs and outputs so any edit to the file fails verification.

    Methods and audit

    Travel, equity and assumptions

    Each neighborhood centroid travels to one synthetic hospital, grocery store, high school and job center. Dijkstra finds the fastest trips through bidirectional walking streets and directional onboard states. Expected wait is half the headway per boarding. Timetable mode boards the next recurring departure, including after a transfer. Headways are cycle time divided by buses. Rail service is fixed and excluded from the municipal bus budget.

    Coverage is the share of resident and destination pairs within 30 minutes, weighted by population. Access for one neighborhood is 1 divided by (1 + its mean trip in minutes / 30). The population-weighted Gini measures inequality of that access score, from zero for equal access toward one for unequal access. Demographic shares describe neighborhoods and do not change the routing weights.

    The baseline concentrates buses on the peak corridor. The exact search compares every feasible fleet allocation. Its score is mean trip minutes + 25 × Gini + 0.45 × mean heat-exposed wait minutes. Each candidate gets its highest-savings shelters at stops on lines whose fleets can change; they cut modeled exposure by 80% without making a trip faster. Individual indicators can worsen when the combined score improves. The hospital minimum uses all feasible schedules, not just the chosen plan.

    At 35°C and above, walking is slower and sunny streets cost extra time. The heat coefficients and stop shade assumptions are exploratory, not medical thresholds or calibrated estimates. Each timetable repeats during the modeled service period; daily hours price staffing, and hour-of-day controls phase rather than service closures. Trips omit fares, crowding, traffic variation, paratransit and street-level mobility barriers.
